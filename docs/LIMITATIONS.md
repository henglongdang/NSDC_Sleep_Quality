# Limitations

1. The observation period is short, and most students contribute roughly two weeks of data.
2. The sample is small and comes from nonrandom recruitment.
3. Participant-day records are repeated measurements, not independent students.
4. Behaviours, stress, screen time, planned bedtime, and sleep quality are self-reported.
5. The observed stress scale conflicts with the stated 1-5 instrument. This weakens cross-student comparisons.
6. The dataset contains missing IDs, duplicate day labels, out-of-range study days, and mixed response formats.
7. Wearable measurements are available for a subset and may reflect device ownership or reporting practices.
8. Time-of-day entries can be ambiguous around AM/PM and midnight. The pipeline documents a limited correction rule, but cannot validate every entry.
9. Screen-time estimates can exceed the stated 60-minute window and include ambiguous ranges.
10. Target-label mapping for written sleep-quality labels is reasonable but subjective. Numeric-only performance remains similar, but that sensitivity has only 175 held-out rows.
11. The models may overfit modest samples even with grouped and chronological evaluation.
12. The unseen-student and known-student scenarios answer different questions. A result in one should not be generalized to the other.
13. Wearable associations have smaller and selective samples. They do not apply to nonwearable participants by default.
14. Unmeasured factors such as illness, housing, deadlines, medications, noise, and personal sleep needs may confound observed relationships.
15. Observational associations do not show that changing caffeine, screen use, stress, or exercise would change sleep quality.
